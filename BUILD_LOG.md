## Pick: A (135 chars)

> The hospice nurse charted my mother's last breath at 3 a.m., then crossed it out, because the woman in the bed never stopped breathing.

Why A over the rest: B is a clever twist but reads as a joke setup more than dread;
C is atmospheric but the payoff (a logbook entry) is a bureaucratic scare, not a
visceral one; D leans on the well-worn baby-monitor trope; E is sad more than
frightening; F is two scares stapled together and the chair image is tired. A earns
its dread: the charting and the crossing-out are concrete, clinical details, the
turn is withheld (who is breathing in that bed now?), and "never stopped breathing"
lands the blow as the last words.

Revision pass: no better line emerged; A kept as drafted. One wording considered
and rejected: ending "...kept breathing until sunrise" — softer last word than
"never stopped breathing".
